## 1. Cost of inaction too low

**Spoken (20 s):** "We chose not to inflate it. We only count deterioration our projects can actually absorb: $1.32m present value, three named exposures. And the decision does not need it: projects alone are +$5.62m. If you believe inaction costs more, the case gets stronger, not weaker."

**Backup arguments (use two, not all):**
1. **The counterfactual is not "no AI".** TotalEnergies already runs 10,000+ models, ~100 Digital Factory solutions, and just committed >€100m to Mistral (15 Sep 2026). Our programme is the last-mile delivery fix on top. So the loss from not acting is *lost acceleration*, small by construction.
2. **Sector.** Hydrocarbons and refining are price-taker, asset-based businesses (11.2 Gboe reserves, >12 years; production cost $5.0/boe; ROACE 12.6%; gearing 9.1% at 30 Sep 2026). AI changes the cost curve, not market structure: no AI-native entrant takes share from a refinery or a deepwater field. Peers have the same tools, so the pressure is margin and cost, which is what our three exposures measure.
3. **Delay value is small because benefits are deferred, not lost** ($0.65m = 9% of base NPV). That is also why the *option* logic works: Tranche 1 is $2.90m, so we buy information cheaply instead of burning a platform.
4. **We refuse invented erosion.** An earlier version carried a $150m customer pool and 50% avoidance; reviews found no qualifying pool or causal pathway, so it was removed (`work/research/overlap_and_inaction_review.md`). The report also says peer announcements (Equinor $120m cumulative PdM, BP well planning) do not prove a TotalEnergies gap.
5. **Talent** is a real but unquantified exposure (URD p. 318 names competition for skills). We track it as a leading indicator, not a cash number.

**Do not say:** "competitors will take our market share" (no evidence), "AI is existential for TotalEnergies".

**Trap follow-up: "If inaction is so cheap, why now?"** → "It is a low-regret, gated option, not a burning platform. Platforms (AspenTech, Cognite) roll out to 2027–28 and the Digital Line was created in Sept 2025; the window to set up delivery is now, and the first tranche is 0.01% of annual operating cash flow ($27.8bn FY2025)."

## 2. Gap between downside (−$1.30m) and base (+$7.11m); optimism of returns

**Spoken:** "The $8.4m gap is almost entirely benefits timing and size. Present value of benefits falls from $29.9m to $20.0m; costs rise by under $1m. We show it, we do not hide it. Our margin of safety: the portfolio breaks even if benefits come in 30% below base."

**Decomposition** *[recomputed]*: PV at 10%, base → downside: benefits 29.9 → 20.0 (−9.9); costs 18.2 → 18.9 (+0.8); tax 4.6 → 2.4 (+2.2 cushion). Net −8.4. About 85% of the gap is benefits (ramp, 80–90% factor, P12 receipts removed).

**Why returns look high, honestly:**
- IRR is on a **small, capital-light base**: capex only $5.69m (26% of envelope); the rest is opex, expensed and tax-deductible. Platforms are reused (AspenTech, Cognite): we pay marginal integration, not the platform.
- **No terminal value**; run-rate benefit in Y5 is $13.5m/year against $2.8m run cost (4.8×) but years 6+ count for nothing.
- Each benefit is a **small percentage** of a very large base (0.45% throughput, 4% waste × 25%, 2% fuel). Easy to believe, hard to verify, hence Finance-verified gates.
- **Not reliant on H3:** NPV excluding P12 is +$6.93m; excluding P03 +$5.14m; excluding P12, P03 and P13 +$4.45m *[recomputed]*. P12 receipts ($4.4m, uncontracted) add only +$0.18m NPV.

**Sensitivity table** *[recomputed, base case, NPV at 10%]*:

| Shock | NPV $m |
|---|---:|
| Base | +7.11 |
| Benefits −20% | +2.55 |
| Benefits −30% | +0.25 (break-even ≈ −30%) |
| Benefits −40% | −2.06 |
| Benefits −50% | −4.39 |
| Costs +20% | +4.39 |
| Costs +50% | +0.30 (break-even ≈ +50%) |
| Costs +100% | −6.52 |

**Expected value robustness** *[recomputed]*: weights 50/30/20 → +$3.94m; 60/30/10 → +$2.58m. Replace the downside with the severe stress at 30% → **+$3.0m**; at 50% severe → −$0.4m. So EV stays positive unless the severe case is more likely than not.

**Admit if pressed:** weights 30/50/20 are judgemental; all benefit inputs are assumptions; cost overruns of +5–10% are mild (Flyvbjerg and Budzier found a 27% average overrun on 1,471 IT projects, and about 1 in 6 are extreme tail cases near +200%; [arXiv:1304.0265](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.0265)). Our answer is the +50% break-even and the gates that stop spend, not a claim that overruns will not happen. Project risks are **correlated** (one adoption bottleneck), so the downside should be read as a joint event, not diversified.

**Killer question (from our own audit): "If it is that easy, why haven't my teams done it?"**
→ "Because the constraint is not technology. Time saved is not cash saved: rig days only count if the contract lowers the payment (P03), a prediction only counts if a barrel is sold (P01), markdowns only count if incremental (P13). We fund the organisation that turns solutions into measured cash, and each case names the weakest assumption and its break-even."

## 3. "Your projects are mostly opex, not capex": use it, but know the other edge

**For us:** low capital at risk ($5.69m capex, Y0–Y1 only), little stranded asset if we stop, costs are reversible and can be paused at each gate; 40% then 60% of run cost moves to branches.
**Against us (expect it):** recurring cost is **55% of the envelope ($12.12m)**; from Y4 it is a permanent $2.7–2.8m/year; "stopping" does not recover sunk spend. Answer: Gate 2 requires verified annual benefits ≥ run costs and 60% branch co-funding; projects a branch will not co-fund do not continue.
Also: opex-heavy means savings can be absorbed by running costs of the tool itself, which is why every case counts net incremental cash, after run cost and tax.

## 4. "Why this budget and not half?"

**Spoken:** "Half is our fallback and Gate 0 gets us there naturally. Complementary cases (P02, P04, P05, P08, P09) are released only after a Finance-signed baseline. But half gives up $2.3m of expected NPV and drops E01, the model register that the AI Act timeline (high-risk provisions apply from 2 Dec 2027) makes necessary."

Numbers: four featured cases cost $11.35m (51.7%); base IRR 34.8%, downside 6.4%, expected NPV +$3.35m vs +$5.62m full; complementary cases individually: P04 +$1.05m, P08 +$0.99m, P02 +$0.99m, P05 +$0.42m, P09 +$0.21m; E01 −$0.76m (it is a cost, benefit sits in other cases).
**Honest weak point:** higher IRR for half is a capital-efficiency fact; we choose absolute NPV and breadth. Also half excludes E01 and "relies on existing controls".
**Do not say:** "half destroys strategic coherence" (the course script); our own numbers show half still works. Say "half is viable, and it is what a failed Gate 0 would deliver".

## 5. Sector, "already on good rails", "why is this on my agenda?"

- Scale check: programme $21.94m over 6 years vs FY2025 CFFO $27.8bn and net investments $17.1bn: ~0.1%. It is not a strategy bet; it is a proof of delivery model. Mistral alone is >€100m.
- Position: strategy, data and delivery experience score 3–3.5 in our outside-in readiness; organisation and governance 2.5. We do not claim the group is behind; we claim value is held back by *how project work is organised*.
- Capital discipline: capex cut by $1bn/year, $12.5bn savings plan 2026–2030, payout >40% of cash flow. We fit inside: small, gated, branch co-funded.
- **Do not claim AI contributes to the $12.5bn plan**: the levers are undisclosed (open question Q06). If asked "is this already in my savings plan?": "Baselines signed by Finance exclude already-planned savings; only incremental cash counts. We cannot verify the overlap from public data, that is exactly what Gate 0 checks."
- Counter-risk: "not disrupted" invites "then don't spend". Always pair with "low-regret, gated".

## 6. Bottleneck and trio

- Evidence: public (URD p. 138, risk of pace of organisational and skills adaptation; Digital Line created Sept 2025) plus one local observation (CLM-074). **Say "hypothesis" and "our insider's observation in one branch".**
- It is tested, not assumed: lead time to first live use, interval between reviews, projects per lead, active users. If data access or a contract is the real constraint, we change the intervention.
- Cost sits inside project envelopes (talent and change $4.07m of $16.40m Y0–Y3). No new organisation, no new platform.
- Q: "What does the trio add versus today?" → "We do not book a separate benefit; project returns are standalone and the downside already assumes slower adoption. The trio is how we get the ramps in the base case; the 90-day baseline of current lead times is our control."
- Dual reporting friction is named as a risk with an escalation (sponsor within a week).

## 7. Gates and kill rules

- **Kill rule format (course Q2):** metric + threshold + date + owner + action. Use slide 20: e.g. <40% eligible users active at month 6 → remediate or stop within 30 days (branch sponsor + CHRO); <60% decisions in tool at month 12 → no expansion; no Finance-verified benefit or negative prospective NPV at month 12 → pause/redesign/stop (CFO + sponsor); P03 contract does not allow capture at Gate 0 → stop that scope; P12 no IP clearance / two developers by Y2 → stop, $0.10m closure.
- **Weak point:** Gate 1 requires ≥3 cases with Finance-verified incremental benefit at month 12, while the base ramps are 0% (P03, P09, P12) or 20% (P01, P04, P08) in Y1. Answer: the three candidates are P13 (50% ramp in Y1), P01 and P05 (20–25%), measured against pre-registered baselines on pilot cohorts; if fewer than three qualify, Gate 1 pauses the failing cases, which is the stated design.
- **Weak point:** day-90 Gate 0 includes employee consultation; in France that can take longer. Answer: consultation starts in the first 90 days; a case that is not ready is simply not released, its envelope is held.
- Worst case with gates: if everything fails at month 12, cash out is Y0+Y1 ≈ $11–13.7m gross trough (−$11.5m downside; −$13.7m severe) *[recomputed]*; Y2 onwards is not released.

## 8. Case-level attacks

**P01 refining.** Assumptions: 250 kb/d two sites, 0.45% recovered, 70% sold, $6/bbl, $0.15m repairs. NPV zero at 57.6% of benefit. If asked "$6/bbl is low": Q3 2026 ERM marker was $33.2/b (a market marker, not our realised margin), so $6 is conservative, but cyclical. External benchmark: published predictive-maintenance figures (20–30% maintenance cost, 10–20% downtime reduction) come largely from vendors or unclear attribution; McKinsey notes prerequisites (data history, sensors, high downtime value). Treat as context only, never as proof. Equinor's $120m is cumulative since 2020, including partners. "Why hasn't it been done?" → AspenTech platform is only rolling out to 2027–28.

**P03 drilling.** Weakest assumption: contract. 80% economic share × 75% payment avoidability. At 40% share the benefit halves to $1.44m, below $1.66m break-even; hence Gate 0 contract test before Y1 spend. Mero is Petrobras-operated (TotalEnergies 19.3%), so we do not assume unilateral deployment: scope is "operated rigs". 6 avoidable days per rig per year is a small slice.

**P13 retail.** €1.0m for 100 stations (insider calibration). Without markdowns (€0.25m), benefit €0.385m < break-even €0.446m. Mitigation: markdowns measured first on separate SKU cohorts; forecast-only fallback; labels must beat forecast-only.

**P12 e-SAF.** H3 with no invented payback; conditional receipts $0.60m/$1.50m/$2.30m in Y3–Y5 are not contracted; base NPV contribution +$0.18m; downside stops at Y2 with $0.10m closure. "Why H3 at only 10%?" → bounded option, Mistral already funds subsurface so we avoid duplication.

**Horizon mix 70/20/10.** Deliberate tilt to H1 for long-cycle industrial assets (course "deviate on purpose"): 70.5/19.4/10.2 on one-time investment, 65.6/21.4/13.0 on total spend.

**Discount rate.** 10% = 7.0% published post-tax impairment WACC (FY2025) + 3-point AI premium; project hurdles 9–12%. Premium is our assumption. Sensitivity ±1pt of WACC is in the policy note. IRR of expected cash flows (27.8%) is not an expected IRR.
