# Portfolio cost of inaction — illustrative decision overlay

**Working academic scenario, 10 November 2026.** All quantities below are explicit team assumptions, not observed TotalEnergies facts or a forecast. This is a separate overlay; the existing spreadsheets and their project assumptions are unchanged. The continuation case retains current AI, retention, commercial and innovation initiatives. Comparisons use USD millions, nominal post-tax cash, Y0–Y5 and a common 10% discount rate; no terminal value.

## Evidence and calibration limits

**Verified fact:** TotalEnergies' 2025 Universal Registration Document, PDF and printed p. 318, identifies competitiveness and innovation risks linked to inadequate skills management and identifies competing technology and energy sectors as a challenge to attracting and retaining key skills ([CLM-080](../../sources/register.md)). On p. 42 the company describes its existing Digital Line and Digital Factory, which remain in the continuation case. These statements support the relevance of the risk categories, **not** the assumed number of departures, the cost of a departure, a specific AI programme's retention effect, or any market-share erosion.

**Disconfirming evidence and scale:** the company is already deploying digital solutions; it is not assumed to do nothing about AI overall. Equinor's company-reported 2025 AI value includes partners and an undisclosed method ([CLM-035](../../sources/register.md)); it does not establish an equivalent loss for TotalEnergies. The hypothetical $150m revenue pool equals approximately **0.075%** of TotalEnergies' FY2025 $201,196m external sales ([CLM-031](../../sources/register.md)); this arithmetic only describes scale, not the identity, margin, ownership or vulnerability of that pool. Neither its size nor the 50% mitigation effect has an external source. Exposure and assumed mitigation were selected to explore a positive adjusted downside; they cannot be represented as independent evidence or statistically calibrated inputs.

## Relative scale compared with the course example

The course's **fictional MaisonRetail** case (course PDF pp. 120, 126 and 131–133) has a €30m three-year envelope, another €2m of cost in each of Y4 and Y5, and approximately €8.0m of five-year no-programme erosion PV at 12%. Our full portfolio has $27.6371m gross Y0–Y5 spending and $3.143m of illustrative no-programme erosion PV at 10%. These ratios are dimensionless within each case; they do not convert euro to dollar.

| Avoided erosion PV / gross cost | Course bridge assumes the full €8m is avoided; €8m / €34m = **23.5%** | Assumed 50% avoided; $1.571m / $27.6371m = **5.7%** | Avoidance effects are assumptions, not comparable demonstrated impacts. |

Re-discounting our same assumed loss path at the course's 12% gives **$2.927m**, or **10.6%** of our gross ceiling. This isolates the discount-rate difference only; it does not align cash timing, risk, tax treatment, corporate scope or the probability of prevention. The hypothetical $150m revenue exposure is 0.075% of TotalEnergies' FY2025 external sales; the course's €8m PV compared with its fictional ~€2bn annual revenue is 0.4%, but group-wide sales are not the eligible revenue pool and PV-to-one-year-revenue is only a scale reference. Smaller ratios do not demonstrate that the exposure, erosion trajectory or 50% effect is credible. Specifically, the customer pools and funded interventions are not identified, and the full overlay may overlap existing project benefits. The comparison is a sanity check against the teaching example, **not independent validation or a benchmark for calibrating a positive downside**.

The overlay applies equally to all scenarios as a simplifying assumption, including the gated downside: it assumes the relevant measures still run and achieve the same 50% effect after H3 stops. That is uncertain and may overstate downside value. Scenario weights are subjective; weighted IRR is calculated from weighted annual cash, not averaged IRRs. Base adjusted cash is −3.697 / −8.140 / +2.022 / +4.980 / +7.755 / +9.005. At 0%, 25%, 50%, 75% avoidance, avoided-loss PV is 0 / +0.786 / +1.571 / +2.357 respectively; all other inputs fixed. Zero avoidance restores base NPV +3.633 and weighted NPV +3.303. The course case numbers are fictional and not copied.

The common-rate numbers differ from the workbook's sum of NPVs at project-specific rates and must not be combined with it. Recalculate the workbook and independently inspect costs, overlap, actual ownership and the feasibility of the effects. The $276.02m estimate remains withdrawn. Downside is not a loss cap, and later H3 value is not included.


## Revision and downside break-even sensitivity

The competitive revenue exposure was increased from $100m to $150m at the user’s request to explore a larger cost of inaction; this is a scenario choice, not evidence that the exposure is $150m. At the common 10% rate, the original unrounded cached downside NPV is −$1.374m and the revised no-programme erosion has a PV of $3.143m. Holding all else fixed, break-even avoidance is approximately **43.7%**. At 50% avoidance the downside NPV is **+$0.197m**, with IRR approximately **10.6%**. Under the prior $100m exposure and 50% avoidance, it was −$0.146m. These are sensitivities, not evidence that the programme can prevent 50% of the losses. The positive result depends on the exposure, attributable causal effect, cost coverage and non-overlap assumptions, and on relevant interventions continuing in the gated downside. No rounding-based result near zero is a robust safety margin. The separate severe stress remains −$11.624m before any inaction adjustment and cannot be described as a bounded worst-case loss.
