# Half-budget response

 If only half the budget is available

Half of the selected gross envelope is **$8.144m**. Retain P01, P05, P09, E01 and the initial P12 experiment through Y2, with $0.10m closure/transfer in Y3. This costs **$7.460m**, leaving **$0.684m** unallocated. P02, P03 and P13 are deferred in this constrained alternative. No proportional haircut to benefits is used; retained scopes retain their original costs and benefits. P12 receives no automatic continuation beyond the funded experiment.

| Scenario | NPV at 10%, USD m | IRR |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Downside | -3.367 | -20.2% |
| Base | +0.732 | 17.3% |
| Upside | +2.183 | 31.5% |
| Weighted | +0.070 | 10.7% |
| Severe stress | -4.100 | -28.0% |


The half-budget weighted NPV is only marginally positive. This is a feasible constrained response, not equally attractive performance at half cost. It sacrifices upstream/drilling and retail learning and most of the full portfolio's expected net value. Its remaining headroom is not a forecast saving. The H1/H2/H3 mix remains present, while the H3 commitment is limited to an interpretable experiment. Dependencies and shared governance costs are retained, not halved.

**Why the half-budget portfolio is not simply the four featured cases.** The featured cases were selected to explain the strategy and its different decision mechanisms, not to fit a 50% spending limit. P01 + P03 + P12 + P13 cost **$11.347m**; retaining the required E01 controls brings that to **$12.287m**, or **75.4%** of the full envelope, against **$8.144m** available. Even limiting P12 to Y0–Y2 plus closure gives **$11.037m**. Keeping all four at half budget would therefore require redesigned asset/store scopes, fixed-versus-variable costs and benefit ramps. Proportional scaling would be misleading. The constrained alternative instead preserves complete, costed operating cases and one bounded H3 experiment. It is a feasible response, not a claim of an optimized subset across all possible scope redesigns.
