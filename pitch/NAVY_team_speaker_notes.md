**How to use these notes**

- Use the script to rehearse. On the day, say it in your own words rather than reading it out.
- Say **bold numbers** exactly as written. They match the deck, the memo and the report.
- Sentences in [square brackets] are optional. Cut them if you are behind the clock.
- "If challenged" lines are short answers for the Q&A. Don't say them during the pitch.
- Slides 19–27 are the appendix. Don't present them; open one only when a question calls for it.

## Part 1 · Where we stand (Alexandre)

### Slide 4 · Section divider

**Alexandre · 0:05 · clock 1:25 → 1:30**

Say nothing here; move straight to the next slide.

### Slide 5 · "Strong foundations: the organisation of delivery holds value back"

**Alexandre · 1:30 · clock 1:30 → 3:00**

> We scored TotalEnergies on five readiness dimensions, from 1 to 5. It is an outside-in view from public sources and our field experience, not an internal audit.
>
> The foundations are strong. You run more than **10,000 live AI models**, and the Digital Factory has built about **100 solutions** since 2020. AI delivery experience scores **3.5**; strategy and technology score **3**. That puts the group at the **implementing stage, close to scaling**.
>
> Two dimensions lag at **2.5**: organisation and culture, and governance. We name organisation as the bottleneck, and more precisely how project work is organised. Today each AI project is split across **four people on four reporting lines**. Nobody owns the whole project, so iterations are slow and each lead steers only a few projects.
>
> [Governance is a real gap too, but it doesn't stop projects from moving. We fund it directly in the portfolio with a shared register of models, baselines and controls.]
>
> To be transparent: one of us saw this split first-hand in one branch. It matches the group's own disclosed risk on the pace of organisational change, and the first pilots will measure how widespread it is.
>
> [The orange circles show where we aim to be after Gate 2: organisation from 2.5 to 3.5.] The constraint is not technology and not ownership. It is how delivery is organised.

**If challenged**

- *"Your bottleneck rests on one branch."* Yes, it's a local observation and we say so. In the first 90 days we measure the time between progress reviews, the lead time from approval to first live use, and the number of projects per lead. If the real blocker turns out to be data access or contracts, we adjust instead of adding teams.
- *"Why not governance as the bottleneck?"* Weak governance slows releases, but it doesn't make a working project slow. We fund it anyway (E01, slide 27) and no release happens without the controls.

### Slide 6 · "Keep the hub; give each project a dedicated trio"

**Alexandre · 1:50 · clock 3:00 → 4:50**

> So what do we change? Not the model: the way each project team works.
>
> We keep the hub-and-spoke model you already have. The hub is the Digital Line and the Digital Factory: platforms, standards and AI expertise. The spokes are the branches: use cases and results. For a group this size, with this safety exposure and scarce AI talent, it is the right model. [A pure centre of excellence would queue work; a federated model would duplicate tools and weaken control of risk.]
>
> The problem is on the left, inside each project: four people, four different managers, and **no common boss**. The project lead owns the objective but directs nobody, so they have to negotiate. The data engineer follows branch IT's priorities, the data scientist from the Digital Factory, TDF, has no financial objective, and the end user can't decide. Everyone works part-time, with their own priorities. The result: progress reviews weeks apart, late user feedback, and few projects per lead.
>
> On the right is our answer: **one dedicated trio per project**. The TDF data scientist, a branch data engineer and a business expert who brings the users' voice into the team. They spend at least half their time on the project, work together every day and report to the project lead on objectives and priorities. Their usual manager keeps standards and careers.
>
> Objectives, budgets, gates and the HSE and legal vetoes don't change. What changes: a question gets settled in days instead of waiting for the next steering meeting, and each project lead can run more projects.
>
> Vincent will now show you what this model will deliver.

**If challenged**

- *"So you're changing the hub-and-spoke model?"* No, we keep it as it is. The change is inside each project: who works together, for how much of their time, and who they report to day to day.
- *"Dual reporting creates conflict."* Each trio member's objectives are agreed jointly before launch: project objectives with the project lead, standards with the line manager. Priority conflicts go to the branch sponsor within a week.
- *"Who pays for the people you pull out of their teams?"* Their time, backfill and training sit inside each project envelope, under the "talent and change" cost line.

## Part 2 · What we fund (Vincent)

### Slide 7 · Section divider

**Vincent · 0:05 · clock 4:50 → 4:55**

> Thank you, Alexandre.

### Slide 8 · "Ten initiatives, 70/20/10 by design: H1 pays back first"

**Vincent · 1:10 · clock 4:55 → 6:05**

> We fund ten initiatives: **$9.82 million** of one-time investment, and **$21.94 million** over five years once running costs are included.
>
> On this map, each bubble is one initiative. Left to right is how fast it pays back; bottom to top is how much cash it brings each year at full scale; the size is what it costs upfront; the colour is the horizon.
>
> The split is deliberate: **70.5% H1, 19.4% H2, 10.2% H3**, which is the 70/20/10 rule. H1, in dark blue, means reliability and cost gains on assets you already run: refinery maintenance, renewable fleet performance, customer retention. These pay back within roughly three years and fund the J-curve. H2 means drilling and LNG voyages, where workflows cross functions and contracts. H3 is one bounded option, e-SAF technology licensing, which stops if it fails its gates.
>
> [We build no proprietary model. Every case buys or blends market tools with our own data and expertise.]
>
> Each horizon has a role and a stop rule. Let me take you through three cases.

**If challenged**

- *"Only $22 million, for a group this size?"* It is deliberately small. Its job is to prove that dedicated trios deliver measured cash faster. If Gate 2 confirms it, the model extends to more sites through separate investment cases.
- *"Why so much H1?"* The group runs long-lived industrial assets, and reliability gains there pay back fastest and fund the trough. If H3 fails, it falls back to its sunk cost.

### Slide 9 · P13 retail

**Vincent · 0:55 · clock 6:05 → 7:00**

> First case: French retail. In **100 large company-operated service stations**, we forecast demand better and use electronic shelf labels to cut prices on products close to their expiry date, so they get sold instead of thrown away. [We buy the labels and blend the forecasting.]
>
> It costs **€1.0 million** and brings **€0.64 million a year** at full scale, through less waste, markdown margin and fewer stock-outs. Base NPV is **+€0.49 million** at a 9% hurdle, the IRR is **30%**, and payback is **2.9 years**, or 3.8 in the downside.
>
> The weakest assumption is the markdowns. If the labels add nothing on top of better forecasting, the case falls below break-even. So we test that first, with a matched-store trial at month 12. If labels don't beat forecasting alone, we keep the cheaper forecasting and stop buying hardware.

**If challenged**

- *"Why euros?"* It is a French case; the portfolio converts it at $1.1165 per euro.
- *"Where does €0.64m come from?"* Waste €0.245m, markdowns €0.250m and stock-outs €0.140m (slide 23). Without the markdown stream the benefit drops to €0.385m, below the €0.446m break-even.

### Slide 10 · P01 refining

**Vincent · 0:55 · clock 7:00 → 7:55**

> Second case: predictive maintenance in two refineries. We blend vendor tools with site expertise to catch failures on critical rotating equipment before they stop a unit.
>
> We invest **$1.4 million** for **$1.87 million a year** at full scale. The formula: we assume 250,000 barrels a day across the two sites, 0.45% more throughput, 70% of it sold at $6 a barrel, plus avoided emergency repairs. Base NPV is **+$1.62 million**, the IRR **47.6%**, and payback **2.6 years**.
>
> The throughput gain is an assumption, not a measurement, so we stress-tested it: the NPV stays positive down to **58%** of that benefit.
>
> One rule applies: a good prediction score is not a return. We count only barrels actually sold and repair invoices actually avoided.

**If challenged**

- *"What do competitors get?"* Equinor reports $120 million of predictive-maintenance value since 2020, cumulative and including partners. That shows it can be done; it doesn't prove our annual number.
- *"You already deploy AspenTech."* Yes. The existing tools and maintenance plans are the baseline, and we fund only the increment on top of them.

### Slide 11 · P03 drilling

**Vincent · 0:55 · clock 7:55 → 8:50**

> Third, an H2 case: a drilling advisory tool on two operated deepwater rigs. The tool advises and the drilling engineers keep full authority; there is no autonomous steering.
>
> We invest **$1.4 million** for **$2.88 million a year** at full scale: six rig days saved per rig per year, at $400,000 a day, with 60% of that actually captured. Base NPV is **+$1.86 million** at an 11% hurdle, the IRR **43.2%**, and payback **3.1 years**.
>
> The catch is that time saved is not cash saved. Saved days count only if the rig contract lets us pay less. That is the very first test, at Gate 0, before any year-one spend. If the contract doesn't allow it, we stop.
>
> Yoni will now show you what the portfolio returns as a whole.

**If challenged**

- *"If it's that easy, why haven't my drilling teams done it?"* They may have done part of it, which is why existing tools are our baseline and we count only the increment. What is usually missing is not the tool but a contract clause that turns saved days into lower payments. Gate 0 tests exactly that.

## Part 3 · What it returns (Yoni)

### Slide 12 · Section divider

**Yoni · 0:05 · clock 8:50 → 8:55**

> Thank you, Vincent.

### Slide 13 · "Build in Y1, then about $2.8m a year of run cost from Y4"

**Yoni · 0:55 · clock 8:55 → 9:50**

> This is where the money goes: **$21.94 million** over five years, from now to Y5.
>
> Capital expenditure is **$5.69 million**, all of it in Y0 and Y1: equipment, software and the shelf labels. Everything else is operating cost. Y1 is the build year at **$8.55 million**, mostly integration and talent, a quarter each, and it is only released at Gate 0.
>
> From Y4 the programme runs at about **$2.8 million a year** for software, cloud, support and model operations. These run costs are budgeted from day one rather than discovered later. The branches take them over: they co-fund **40%** in Y2 and **60%** from Y3. [If a branch won't co-fund its project, that tells us the project shouldn't continue.]

**If challenged**

- *"Why five years, not three?"* Long-cycle industrial assets: maintenance, drilling and retail benefits ramp over two to three years. On a three-year view, spend is $16.40 million (slide 22).

### Slide 14 · "Expected NPV +$5.62m at 10%; not investing costs $1.32m more"

**Yoni · 1:20 · clock 9:50 → 11:10**

> Is it worth it? We modelled three scenarios on the same cost base.
>
> The base case gives **+$7.11 million** of NPV. The upside, with faster adoption and 15% more benefits, gives **+$12.28 million**. The downside assumes slower adoption, 80 to 90% of benefits, costs 5 to 10% over plan and the e-SAF option stopped, and gives **−$1.30 million**.
>
> We weight them 30% downside, 50% base and 20% upside, so the downside deliberately counts more than the upside. The expected NPV is then **+$5.62 million** and the IRR **27.8%**, against a **10%** hurdle. That hurdle is TotalEnergies' own **7% WACC** from its 2025 impairment tests, plus **3 points** for AI execution risk.
>
> On the bridge, you can see that not investing is not neutral. Refining margins at risk, rig rates and customer churn are all expected to worsen, and our projects absorb part of that, worth **$1.32 million**. So investing is worth **$6.94 million** compared with doing nothing. [And waiting one year costs about **$0.65 million** of NPV.]
>
> The downside falls below the hurdle, and we say so openly, but no scenario loses cash.

**If challenged**

- *"Why 30/50/20?"* It is a conservative weighting: the downside counts more than the upside. The full table is on slide 21.
- *"Is the cost of inaction in your NPV?"* No. Project NPVs exclude it, and we track the three exposures as leading indicators.
- *"Terminal value?"* None. Cash flows stop at Y5, so if benefits continue, the returns are understated.

### Slide 15 · "Costs land first; each release needs evidence"

**Yoni · 1:05 · clock 11:10 → 12:15**

> This is the J-curve, in cash. Red bars are what we spend, green bars what we earn, and the line is cumulative cash.
>
> Costs land first. Cash bottoms out at **−$9.8 million** at the end of Y1. Then benefits take over: **$5.2 million** in Y2, rising to **$13.5 million** a year by Y5. We break even after **3.3 years**.
>
> That trough is why we stage the money. You approve **$2.90 million** now. At Gate 0, around day 90, the **$8.55 million** build year is released case by case, once each trio is staffed and Finance has signed the baseline. At month 12, Gate 1 releases **$2.31 million** against verified benefits. At month 24, Gate 2 funds the run years only if benefits cover run costs.
>
> Maximum exposure before the first gate is **$2.90 million**. Nahel will now explain how we make sure the tools actually get used.

**If challenged**

- *"Worst-case trough?"* Down to −$11.5 million in the downside, with break-even after about 4.6 years. In expectation it is −$10.2 million and 3.4 years.

## Part 4 · How we make it stick (Nahel)

### Slide 16 · Section divider

**Nahel · 0:05 · clock 12:15 → 12:20**

> Thank you, Yoni.

### Slide 17 · "Adoption measured step by step; four risks, four named owners"

**Nahel · 1:30 · clock 12:20 → 13:50**

> A tool nobody uses returns nothing. So we measure adoption as a staircase, and each step has a number.
>
> Months 0 to 6 measure usage: at least **40%** of eligible users active. If we are below that at month 6, we fix it or stop within 30 days.
>
> Months 6 to 12 measure behaviour: at least **60%** of eligible decisions recorded through the tool. Below that at month 12, there is no expansion.
>
> From month 12 we measure value: cash verified by Finance against the baseline. [Hours saved don't count; we book no workforce savings just because time is saved.]
>
> To get there, the business expert in each trio brings users into the design. We run weekly sessions with users during pilots, and we train in three tiers: everyone affected, the trios and their managers, and executives. [Operators keep their decision authority and a manual fallback.]
>
> Four risks, four named owners. Specialist shortage: the CHRO and the Digital Factory lead. Managers who won't release their experts: the branch sponsors. Governance lag on legal, privacy or employee consultation: the General Counsel and the DPO. And Board impatience during the J-curve: the CEO and CFO. That is you, holding to the gates rather than cancelling early.
>
> Every stop rule, with its metric, threshold, date and owner, is agreed before launch.

**If challenged**

- *"How will I follow this?"* Through a monthly one-page dashboard showing spend, lead time, active users, verified benefit, risks and the next funding decision, plus quarterly gate reviews.

### Slide 18 · "The ask: approve Tranche 1 today"

**Nahel · 0:50 · clock 13:50 → 14:40**

> So, our ask. Approve the **$21.94 million** envelope as a planning ceiling, not a commitment to deploy every project. And release **Tranche 1, $2.90 million, today**, case by case, after checks on existing solutions, rights and safety.
>
> From then on, evidence decides. At Gate 0, around day 90, **$8.55 million** is released once trios are staffed and baselines are signed. At Gate 1, month 12, **$2.31 million** follows against verified benefit and adoption. At Gate 2, month 24, run funding comes only if benefits cover run costs.
>
> You already have the models, the platforms and the people. This investment makes them pay. We ask for your decision today, 16 November. Thank you; we're happy to take your questions.
