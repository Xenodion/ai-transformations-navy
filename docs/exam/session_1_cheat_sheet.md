## 1. AI readiness: diagnose the firm

Rate the firm on 5 pillars, place it on 5 stages, then name the **bottleneck pillar**. That is the readiness assessment the oral project expects too.

### The 5 pillars of AI maturity

| Pillar | What it covers | What the slides stress |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Business strategy | How AI supports business goals | Define the business problem first. Link AI to core goals (efficiency, revenue) to win executive support and funding. An investment plan becomes critical once you implement. |
| Technology and data | Data and infrastructure to run AI at scale | Data access and quality come first, from exploring to implementing. Later the focus shifts to data format and relevance. Cloud is what gets you past proof of concept. |
| AI strategy and experience | AI expertise and repeatable processes | Exploring: people and processes. Implementing: match models to the right use cases. Scaling and realizing: repeatable processes for systematic ROI. |
| Organization and culture | Leadership vision, operating model, skills | 100% of realizing-stage leaders communicate an AI vision, against 1% at exploring. Leadership is necessary but does not replace data, process or tech. |
| AI governance | Privacy, security, responsible-AI controls, accountability | Early stages: transparency and explainability, plus third-party data sharing. Scaling and realizing: systems that log use cases, performance and limits, plus PII controls. |

### The 5 readiness stages

| Stage | What it looks like | Value vs cost | Share of firms | Seeing significant value |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Exploring | Learning, experimenting in a few areas | No value, no cost | 16% | 3% |
| Planning | Assessing, defining strategy, running PoCs | No value, low cost | 23% | 12% |
| Implementing | Moving pilots into production (build and run) | Low value, high cost | 36% | 23% |
| Scaling | Scaling AI across the organization | Very high value, high cost | 20% | 61% |
| Realizing | Repeatable, measurable value; AI by design | Value by design | 5% | 96% |

- **Implementing is the trap**: cost peaks before value arrives. Most firms (36%) sit there.
- **Value jumps at scaling** (23% → 61% seeing significant value).
- **Focus shifts from cost to growth** as maturity rises: efficiency dominates early, growth catches up at realizing.

### Why firms fail, and the fix

Top blockers in the survey: skills 19%, data quality 18%, finding the right use cases 17%, culture 14%, infrastructure 12%. Only legal and compliance is small (6%).

The slide's fix: **start with clarity, prioritize high-impact use cases, scale the platform, govern with agility**. The transformation framework puts the **operating model at the centre**, linking three things: business models and use cases, executive strategy and culture, and platform strategy and delivery. The five key topics: pace of transformation, bringing clarity (individual, collective, transformation, visionary), ROI, an AI transformation office, and technology strategy.

## 3. Cases and numbers worth citing

**Klarna** is the course's flagship case: data and AI as the backbone of the operating model, with agents as the execution layer.

- **Feb 2024, GenAI customer service**: 2.3M conversations (two-thirds of chats), the work of 700 full-time agents, 24/7 in 35 languages. Satisfaction on par with humans, 25% fewer repeat inquiries, resolution time down from 11 to under 2 minutes, about USD 40M profit improvement in 2024.
- **Mar 2025, data as the foundation**: about 1,200 SaaS tools shut down (estimated USD 40–80M saved) to end data silos. Revenue per employee grew 4.3×.
- **Jan 2026, agentic as the fabric**: "Systems of record will die in an agentic world." Human support becomes the VIP option.
