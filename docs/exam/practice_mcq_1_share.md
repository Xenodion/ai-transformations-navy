## Answer key

Don't scroll further until you've answered all 30.

**1. C** — Implementing holds 36% of firms. Cost is high and value low, and value only jumps at scaling (23% → 61% of firms see significant value).

**2. D** — **100%** of realizing-stage leaders communicate an AI vision, against 1% at exploring. 61% is a different statistic: the share of *scaling* firms seeing significant value. Don't mix up the two tables.

**3. A** — Rate the 5 pillars, place the firm on a stage, then name the bottleneck pillar. The oral project expects this too.

**4. A** — Organization and culture pillar: leadership is necessary, but it does not replace data, process or tech.

**5. B** — **700** full-time agents (2.3M conversations; resolution time from 11 to under 2 minutes). 1,200 is the trap: it's the number of SaaS tools Klarna shut down in 2025 to end data silos.

**6. D** — Centre of Excellence. The trap, Cost of Equity, is the Ke in the session 3 WACC formula.

**7. B** — The hub owns the platform, rules, model inventory and vendors; the spokes in the BUs choose and deliver use cases.

**8. C** — Regulation decides first, then market spread (several BUs in distinct markets), then maturity.

**9. A** — Same customer, same model, proven tech → H1. "New to us" is not H3.

**10. C** — Returns run the other way: the 10% on H3 earns about 70% of returns (Nagji and Tuff, 2012).

**11. D** — Rule 2: classify every dependency claim, then build a thin slice of the foundation for the first use case.

**12. B** — H1-heavy fails **strategically**: you optimise yourself into irrelevance (margins up, relevance down). H1 is proven tech with payback under about 12 months, so it isn't the financial risk. The portfolio that **fails financially is the H3-heavy one** (moonshots with no P&L impact, so the CFO cuts the budget). Hook: *too much H1 → strategy dies; too much H3 → finance dies.*

**13. A** — ROI = 10 / 50 = 20%. NPV = 49.74 − 50 = −€0.26M: a positive ROI, but value is destroyed.

**14. D** — Cumulative cash: −60, −20, +20 → 2 + 20/40 = 2.5 years. A (+20) is the trap: that's the total net cash, not a time.

**15. C** — Hurdle = WACC + premium = 9 + 4 = 13%.

**16. B** — +2 to +6 points, set by Finance.

**17. D** — 0.25 × (−20) + 0.5 × 8 + 0.25 × 30 = −5 + 4 + 7.5 = +€6.5M. A (+18) is the plain sum without probabilities.

**18. B** — **"Mutually exclusive" means you can only pick one**, so "both" is ruled out. Then rank by **NPV, not IRR**: IRR is a percentage and blind to scale. B creates €12M of value and A only €1M. Keyword trigger: *mutually exclusive → NPV.*

**19. C** — The six are compute/platform, talent, integration, model ops, data acquisition and governance.

**20. A** — Inaction costs money: competitor catch-up, talent exit, regulatory exposure (MaisonRetail: −€8.0M PV). Compare against a decaying status quo, not against zero.

**21. A** — Value dips before it climbs: the intangible investments (process redesign, skills, data) come first (Brynjolfsson, Rock and Syverson, 2021).

**22. C** — 10% algorithms, 20% tech and data, **70% people and processes**: budget the 70%.

**23. D** — There's no open revolt, just quiet avoidance. The early-warning signal is usage stalling in specific teams; the owners are BU heads + HR.

**24. B** — **Desire.** "Is my job safe?" and "what's in it for me?" are the Desire questions. Awareness is "why are we changing?". And since everyone has been trained, Knowledge is covered: **more training cannot fix a missing Desire.** The fix is a manager-led WIIFM ("what's in it for me") plus a redeployment promise.

**25. C** — A kill criterion = metric + threshold + date + owner (+ action). The other three are vague.

**26. A** — Sponsors (Comex), translators (middle managers), adopters (the floor), guardians (CSE, legal, risk, DPO).

**27. D** — Guardians, consulted **before** the pilot. In the Nanterre case (Feb 2025), a court suspended five AI tools because the pilots ran before consultation.

**28. B** — Invert the pyramid: the floor needs the most frequent, most local and most two-way communication.

**29. C** — **Tier 1 = all-employee literacy**, for everyone including contractors: safe use, data rules, AI's limits. It's the EU AI Act Art. 4 baseline. Tier 2 = practitioners (champions, process owners). Tier 3 = executive coaching. Hook: tiers count up from the base of the pyramid, so Tier 1 is the widest group.

**30. A** — **The ask comes first**: amount, return and date, in line one. Architecture diagrams are explicitly banned from the memo ("No architecture diagrams. Numbers a decision-maker can audit."). The golden rule: speak the board's language (capital, risk, advantage, timeline), never the tech.
