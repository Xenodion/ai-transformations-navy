### 3. Priorities stated by the company

The four operational AI priorities named for 2026 are **HSE**, the **digital plant**, **AI for geosciences and reservoirs**, and **modelling for Integrated Power** (forecasting and trading). Source: SRC-011, p. 10. The company frames data as the precondition: "without robust data, there can be no effective AI" (SRC-011, p. 10). In 2025 it "accelerated the development of strong data platforms", "particularly with AspenTech and Cognite" (same page).

## Evidence for and against the legacy draft's claims of silos or an unsuitable CoE

This section reports evidence only. It does not choose an operating model (that is roadmap step 10).

- **Evidence of central coordination:**
  - The Digital Line coordinates "the branches' digital transformation plan" (SRC-002, p. 42).
  - The Digital Factory builds solutions "addressing the digital needs expressed by business units, particularly via strategic programs" (p. 42).
  - Group-wide data platforms are being rolled out: Cognite across all operated upstream assets (SRC-008), AspenTech across industrial sites worldwide (SRC-007).
  - Worldwide methane data is centralised in Pau (p. 29).
  - The Board reviews AI (pp. 211, 223, 225).
- **Evidence of capability inside the businesses:**
  - Integrated Power runs its own ML trading tools (Predictive Layer, Quadra; pp. 109–110).
  - Saft uses AI in R&D (p. 40).
  - Retail runs a GenAI customer assistant (p. 109).
  - The Subsurface R&D line has its own AI tools (p. 42).
  - Observed together with the central units, this looks like a central hub plus capabilities embedded in the segments. That is an observation, not a recommendation.
- **Evidence of limits to scale:**
  - About 100 solutions in about 5.5 years, "progressively being deployed" (p. 42).
  - The industrial data foundation is still being built over 2025–2028 (two- and three-year rollouts; SRC-007, SRC-008). Interpretation: the OneTech president's aim of a foundation "which unifies our industrial data globally" implies that it was not yet unified in 2025.
  - No source publishes the value, adoption or ROI of any AI solution.
- **What remains unproven:** the legacy draft says the group "struggles to industrialise and replicate these solutions beyond the initial pilot site". No source found supports or refutes this, so it stays `Unknown`. The draft also gives "~300 engineers" for the Digital Factory, which is outdated: the URD 2025 says around 500.

## Contradictions and unknowns

- **Digital Factory headcount: 300 vs ~500.** 300 in June 2025 (SRC-009) and Spring 2025 (SRC-012); ~500 "digital and AI experts in France and India" in the URD filed March 2026 (SRC-002). Yet the Summer 2026 newsletter states a goal of "500 engineers by 2027" (SRC-011). Possible reasons: growth plus the India centre, or different scopes (all experts vs AI engineers). Status: `Disputed`. Check with a careers page, a LinkedIn headcount or a 2026 interview.
- **Share of solutions with AI: 60% vs "half".** "60%" (SRC-002, p. 42), "half" (p. 43) and "sixty" of "over a hundred" (SRC-009) all appear. All describe roughly 50–60 AI solutions out of about 100. Use "about half to 60%".
- **Copilot licences.** "First French company to widely implement Copilot" (SRC-012) is the company's claim. The "30,000 licences" figure is unattributed journalism (SRC-014). Keep it `Pending` until an official figure is found.
- **Unknowns:**
  - **Funding:** who pays for Digital Factory solutions, central budget or business units? Needed to compare operating models (step 10).
  - **Decision rights:** who prioritises use cases, and what the "strategic programs" decide.
  - **Model inventory and MLOps** for the "more than 10,000 live AI models"; EU AI Act classification of industrial or energy-network uses.
  - **Deployment rate:** how many of the ~100 solutions are in production, at how many sites, with what adoption.
  - **Value realised:** no dollar figure exists for any internal AI use case.
  - **Size of digital and data teams inside the segments,** outside the Digital Factory.
  - **How the CDO, Digital Line and Digital Factory relate** after the Sept 2025 reorganisation.
  - **The Chief Data Officer role:** a speaker page naming Michel Lutz returned HTTP 404, so this is unverified.
- **Conclusions the sources do not support:** a maturity score, a bottleneck, a performance gap versus competitors, or a judgement that the current organisation is unsuitable.
