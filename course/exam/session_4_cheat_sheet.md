# AI Transformations — Session 4 Cheat Sheet: Change management and executive engagement

As of 31 Oct 2026 · Session of 26 Oct · Source: "Landing the Change" and "Defending an AI strategy to the board" (pages 153–206 of *AI Strategy - Albert School - Master 2026.pdf*)

The course logic runs in order: diagnose readiness → pick the operating model → sequence the portfolio → prove it financially → manage the change. This session covers the last step, then how to defend the whole strategy to a sceptical board. See [session_1_cheat_sheet.md](session_1_cheat_sheet.md) for the exam format and [session_3_cheat_sheet.md](session_3_cheat_sheet.md) for the numbers the memo relies on.

**Syllabus line**: "Design a change-management plan that addresses the four canonical failure modes, with stakeholder mapping, communication cadence, training tiers, and kill criteria." The deliverable is a 3-page change plan appended to the investment case.

**Closing line of the deck: "Technology is the easy part. Land the change."**

## 1. Why change decides value

**10-20-70 (BCG): 10% of AI value comes from algorithms, 20% from technology and data, 70% from people and processes** (skills, workflows, ways of working). **Budget the 70%**: training, process redesign and communication are the project, not an add-on.

| Evidence | Figure | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Projects meeting objectives: excellent vs poor change management | 88% vs 13% (7× more likely) | Prosci |
| Organisations getting zero return from GenAI, despite $30–40bn of enterprise spend | 95% | MIT NANDA, July 2025 |
| GenAI projects predicted to be abandoned after PoC by end-2025 | 30% | Gartner, July 2024 |
| People using AI at work who were trained by their company | 39% | Microsoft & LinkedIn 2024 Work Trend Index |

## 2. What makes AI change different

### Four forces an ERP roll-out never faced

| Force | Evidence | Response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Fear for the job** | 52% of Americans are more concerned than excited about AI (Pew 2023) | State the redeployment promise before launch |
| **Bottom-up pressure** | 78% of AI users bring their own AI tools to work: adoption starts before your programme does | Govern shadow AI; don't just ban it |
| **A faster clock** | AI roadmaps at tech firms now refresh about every month, down from every six | Re-plan quarterly, not yearly |
| **Law and social dialogue** | EU AI Act Art. 4 AI-literacy duty since 2 Feb 2025; in France the works council (CSE) must be consulted | Put consultation on the critical path |

### The perception gap: leaders and the floor are out of sync

- Familiar with AI agents: leaders 67% vs employees 40% (27-point gap).
- Expect AI to accelerate their career: leaders 79% vs employees 67% (12 points).
- Employees already use GenAI **3× more** than their leaders expect.
- **48%** of employees rank training as the #1 factor for GenAI adoption; nearly half feel under-supported (McKinsey, Jan 2025).

### Two classic lenses: the organisation and the person

| Kotter's 8 steps: leading the organisation | ADKAR (Prosci): leading the individual |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Create a sense of urgency | **A**wareness: why are we changing? |
| 2. Build a guiding coalition | **D**esire: what's in it for me? |
| 3. Form a strategic vision | **K**nowledge: how do I do it? |
| 4. Enlist a volunteer army | **A**bility: can I do it in my real work? |
| 5. Enable action by removing barriers | **R**einforcement: will it stick? |
| 6. Generate short-term wins | |
| 7. Sustain acceleration | |
| 8. Institute change | |

### ADKAR applied to an AI roll-out

| Stage | The employee asks | AI-specific lever | Signal to watch |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Awareness | Why AI, and why now? | CEO story tied to a business outcome | Can teams say the "why" in one line? |
| Desire | What's in it for me? Is my job safe? | Manager-led WIIFM + redeployment promise | Opt-in rate; tone of questions |
| Knowledge | How do I use it well and safely? | Role-based training + AI-literacy basics | Training completion by role |
| Ability | Can I use it in my real work? | Practice in the flow of work + champions | Weekly use on priority use cases |
| Reinforcement | Will anyone notice? | Recognition, updated objectives, public wins | Use still rising after 90 days |

**Diagnose the first stuck stage: more training (Knowledge) cannot fix a missing Desire.** Sponsorship effect (Prosci): 79% of projects met objectives with extremely effective sponsors vs 27% with extremely ineffective ones.

### The permafrost: win the waiting third

| ⅓ Ready to change | ⅓ Waiting to see | ⅓ Resistant |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recruit them as champions: early access, a voice, visible credit | Show them peers' wins, fast: **this is where adoption is won or lost** | Listen and remove real risks; don't over-invest in converting them early |

For AI, the narrative is shifting from "change management" to **"change enablement"**: the job is to help people adapt, not to manage them.

## 3. The four failure modes

| Failure mode | What it looks like | Early-warning signal | Mitigation | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Talent flight** | Scarce AI builders and keen early adopters leave for firms that let them build | Attrition or declined offers in AI roles | AI career paths and pay bands (agreed with the CHRO), retention terms, best people on H2/H3 bets not ticket queues, protected learning time | CHRO + Chief AI Officer |
| **2. Middle-management sabotage** | No open revolt: pilots get "rescheduled", old checks kept so work is done twice, team KPIs dip while learning and get defended | Usage stalls in specific teams | Co-design the workflow with managers, relax targets during the learning dip, a manager toolkit and WIIFM script, **make them the messenger, not the audience** | BU heads + HR |
| **3. Governance lag** | Rules, risk and social dialogue trail deployment: shadow AI or a freeze | Shadow AI; legal or CSE backlog | AI council and acceptable-use policy first, risk-tier use cases (fast lane for low risk), consult the CSE early, AI literacy (Art. 4) | General Counsel + CHRO |
| **4. Comex impatience** | Costs land before value; the Comex pulls the plug before year-2 payback | Budget re-opened before month 12 | Pre-agree 12/24/36-month milestones in the memo, report leading indicators monthly (usage → behaviour → value), bank a visible quick win before month 6, stage-gate capital, agree kill criteria upfront | CFO + CEO sponsor |

**Rule: every failure mode in your plan needs a signal visible by month 3 and a named owner.** Ownership test: ask each owner which signal they check every week, and what they do when it turns red.

Supporting facts:
- **Talent flight**: "future-built" firms are 4× more likely to run structured AI-learning programmes with protected learning time, and plan to upskill over 50% of staff (laggards: 20%). 66% of leaders say they won't hire without AI skills.
- **Sabotage**: managers role-model AI in 88% of future-built firms vs 25% of laggards. Trust in agentic AI is 30 points higher when managers model its use. 86% of managers struggle to get their team using AI (Gartner, 1,973 managers).
- **Comex impatience**: the danger zone is **months 6–12**. "Executives are impatient to see returns on GenAI investments" (Rita Sallam, Gartner, July 2024).

### Governance-lag case: CSE v. an insurer (Tribunal judiciaire de Nanterre)

| Date | Event |
| --- | --- |
| 25 Jan 2024 | Management presents a roll-out of new AI tools |
| 17 Jun 2024 | The CSE goes to court to force a consultation |
| 26 Sep 2024 | Consultation formally opened; pilots already running |
| 19 Nov 2024 | The CSE asks the court for more documents |
| 14 Feb 2025 | The judge suspends five AI tools until consultation ends |

**Why it matters: a pilot used by all the staff concerned is a first roll-out, not an experiment.** Penalty: EUR 1,000 per breach for 90 days, plus EUR 5,000 damages to the CSE.

## 4. Stakeholders and communication cadence

### Four stakeholder classes

| Class | Who | What they want | What they fear | What they need from you |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Sponsors** | Comex, board, BU heads | Value on the P&L and a story for the board | A sunk cost and a public failure | A one-page dashboard, milestones, early wins |
| **Translators** | Middle managers, team leads | Hit this quarter's targets | Losing control, status and headcount | Co-design, target relief, a toolkit |
| **Adopters** | Front-line employees, "the floor" | Less drudgery, more interesting work | Job loss, being judged, looking slow | Training, time to practise, a clear promise |
| **Guardians** | Works council (CSE), legal, risk, DPO | Compliance, fairness, no surprises | Unlawful or unsafe use; a bypassed process | Early consultation, documentation, literacy |

### Influence grid (power over adoption × interest; after Mendelow, 1991)

| | Low interest | High interest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **High power** | Keep satisfied: **Guardians** (move them early) | Manage closely: **Sponsors**, **Translators** |
| **Low power** | Monitor | Keep informed: **Adopters** (weak alone, decisive together) |

1. **Guardians sit top-left until something goes wrong**: move them to "manage closely" before the pilot.
2. **Translators hold more power over adoption than the org chart suggests.**
3. **Adopters are weak one by one but decisive together**: involve them, don't just inform them.

### Cadence is usually set up backwards

| Stakeholder | Typical (backwards) | Right cadence | Messenger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sponsors (Comex) | Weekly 40-page steering deck | Monthly 1-page dashboard; quarterly deep-dive | Chief AI Officer |
| Translators (managers) | One cascade e-mail | Weekly 30-min huddle + toolkit | BU head |
| Adopters (the floor) | One launch e-mail | Weekly team huddle, Q&A channel, show-and-tell | Their manager + champions |
| Guardians (CSE, legal, risk) | Told at go-live | Consulted before the pilot; monthly review | CHRO / General Counsel |

**Invert the pyramid: the floor needs the most frequent, most local, most two-way communication.** The missing message: only **7%** of organisations tell staff how to use the time AI saves them (Gartner).

### The messenger matters as much as the message

| The CEO explains | The manager translates | Peers show how |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Why we are doing this" | "What it means for you" | "How we do it here" |
| AI power users are 61% more likely to have heard their CEO explain why GenAI matters | Employees are 1.4× more likely to be frequent agentic-AI users when managers create psychological safety | 3× adoption of priority use cases in a Microsoft sales team after weekly peer-led huddles |

### Build a champions network

1. **Recruit**: volunteers from the "ready third", in every team and shift, not only at HQ.
2. **Equip**: early access, deeper training, a direct line to the AI team.
3. **Activate**: weekly show-and-tell, office hours, a community channel.
4. **Recognise**: public credit, time recognised in objectives, career visibility.

## 5. Training tiers, kill criteria and adoption metrics

### Train in three tiers

| Tier | Who | What |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Tier 1: all-employee literacy** | Everyone, including contractors | Safe use, data rules, AI's limits, prompting basics: your **EU AI Act Art. 4 baseline** |
| **Tier 2: practitioner deep-dive** | Champions, process owners, power users | Redesign workflows, build and evaluate agents, measure value |
| **Tier 3: executive coaching** | Comex and top leaders | Read an AI business case, own the risk, role-model daily use |

**Training must change behaviour, not tick a box.** Embed it in daily work (real tools, real tasks, real feedback, not a once-a-year workshop); design it to be behavioural (leaders model the change, people get the why and early wins); measure it, don't assume it. Microsoft example: 3,000+ engineers went through "Camp AIR", a multi-week accelerator where cross-functional teams learn AI while redesigning a real business challenge. **AI transformation is a team sport.**

### Kill criteria: decide now what will make you stop

**A kill criterion is an explicit, pre-agreed condition under which a failed initiative is retired, and its capital redeployed.**

**Formula: metric + threshold + date + owner + action.** Example: weekly active planners below 40% of target users at month 12 → portfolio owner (CFO) decides to redesign or retire.

| Type | Example threshold |
| --- | --- |
| Adoption | Below 40% of target users active at month 12 |
| Value | No measurable gain against the baseline at month 18 |
| Cost and risk | Run cost above the value created, or an unresolved high-risk incident |

Kill criteria are agreed **before launch** and **never lowered later**. "We will review adoption regularly" is not a kill criterion. "If verified adoption is below the agreed threshold at month 12, pause tranche 2; CFO decides" is.

### Measure usage, then behaviour, then value

| Phase | When | Example metrics |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Usage | Months 0–6 | Active users, sessions per week, priority use cases live |
| 2. Behaviour | Months 6–12 | Proficiency, workflows redesigned, time saved and how it is reinvested |
| 3. Value | Month 12 onwards | P&L KPIs against the pre-launch baseline: cost, revenue, risk |

Prosci: organisations that measure performance against their change objectives are **3× more likely** to meet them.

### The 3-page change plan (appended to the Comex memo)

| Page 1: risk register | Page 2: people and cadence | Page 3: skills and stop rules |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4 failure modes, each with an early-warning signal, mitigation and owner | 4 stakeholder classes, influence grid, cadence calendar, messengers | 3 training tiers, kill criteria, adoption dashboard, 12/24/36 milestones |

Peer-challenge questions: What kills this, and how would we know by month 6? Which managers will resist, and what's in it for them? What if our two best AI engineers leave? Has the works council been consulted, and when? What will the Comex see before value shows up?

## 6. Defending an AI strategy to the board

**The board approves a choice, not an AI roadmap.** Management proposes a specific portfolio, funding envelope, operating model, milestones and accountable owners. The board tests strategic fit, downside exposure, return on capital, controls, and the conditions for continuing. **Translate every technical claim into capital, risk, advantage and timeline.**

**Start with the resolution you want approved**:
1. **What**: approve the 3-year AI investment envelope and the first tranche.
2. **Why now**: protect a strategic position and unlock measurable value.
3. **On what terms**: named owners, gates, risk limits, quarterly reporting.

### The Comex memo

The latest slide (p190) lists eight blocks over two to three pages. An earlier slide (p191) lists seven, folding change management into risks. Learn the eight-block version.

| Block | Content |
| --- | --- |
| **Page 1** | |
| 1. Thesis | Maturity, context, vision, objectives: the decision and why now |
| 2. Portfolio | Three Horizons: what we fund and why this mix; use cases, owners, value levers |
| 3. Investment envelope | 3–5 years: summary, then detail by the six cost categories |
| 4. Scenarios | Downside / base / upside, each with explicit assumptions and probabilities; EV |
| **Page 2** | |
| 5. Financial analysis | EV IRR vs hurdle and WACC; NPV; the cost of inaction |
| 6. Risks and kill criteria | What would make us stop, and when |
| 7. Change management | Operating model, adoption, owners, skilling |
| 8. The ask | Amount, tranche, gates, decision date |

**No architecture diagrams. Numbers a decision-maker can audit.** Writing tips: answer first (the ask in line one); one number per claim; explicit assumptions, no hidden drivers; show the downside honestly.

**Block 1, thesis template**: "Invest [amount] over [period] in [portfolio] to achieve [business outcome], with [downside cap] and [decision gate]." Then explain the why in simple terms (maturity, market conditions, competition, objectives), and end with a call to action: "We expect this meeting to approve…".

### Weak vs strong memo phrasing

| Weak | Stronger |
| --- | --- |
| "We will become an AI-first organisation" / "AI will transform our business" | "EUR 30M over 3 years; ENPV +1.9M at a 12% hurdle; break-even in year 5" |
| "We'll use GPT-class models" | "A platform reused by 10 initiatives, cutting the marginal cost per use case" |
| "Risks are manageable" | "Downside NPV −11.6M; tranche 2 released only if 3 H1 use cases hit baseline +10%" |
| "Please approve the budget" | "Approve tranche 1 (EUR 11M) today; tranche 2 decision at month 12" |

**The test: every strong sentence names an amount, a return, a date or a downside: something a CFO can audit.**

### What each block must show

- **Portfolio (block 2)**: a portfolio, not a list of pilots. H1 efficiency (productivity, cost-to-serve), H2 augmentation (better decisions, service, workflows), H3 new model (options for new revenue and advantage). **70-20-10 is a course heuristic, not a universal benchmark: explain any deviation.**
- **Envelope and hurdle (blocks 3–5)**: make the capital case auditable. Show Y1/Y2/Y3 spend and when value reaches the P&L; compare portfolio IRR with WACC + risk premium; include the cost of inaction.
- **Scenarios (block 4)**: they expose assumptions, not false precision. Downside (adoption lags, costs persist) → loss cap + corrective trigger. Base (measured adoption and benefits) → hurdle test + milestones. Upside (faster scale, new revenue) → capacity and reinvestment option. For each, state the probability, adoption, unit economics, cash-flow timing and a decision response.
- **Risks (block 6)**: name each risk and its control owner.

| Risk | Examples | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Value | No measurable P&L capture | CFO + business owner |
| Delivery | Data, integration or vendor lock-in | CIO / product owner |
| Trust | Privacy, security, bias, unsafe output | Risk / legal / DPO |
| People | Adoption, skills and social dialogue | BU leader / HR |

- **Change (block 7)**: show who changes work, not just who gets tools. Sponsors get a monthly dashboard; translators a weekly huddle and toolkit; adopters practice and local feedback; guardians are consulted before pilots. **Board test: who changes work, what enables adoption, and what evidence releases the next tranche?** Each failure mode has an owner: talent flight → CHRO (attrition, skills coverage); manager resistance → BU head (manager participation); governance lag → legal/risk (approvals, exceptions); Comex impatience → CEO/CFO (adoption, realised value).
- **EU and responsible AI**: put regulatory exposure on the critical path. Keep an AI inventory, classify use cases, document controls, review vendor roles and applicable deadlines. EU AI Act obligations are phased: check the timeline for the specific use case (see [session_2_cheat_sheet.md](session_2_cheat_sheet.md)).

### Make the timeline a contract with evidence

| M12: baseline + first value | M24: scale proven motions | M36: strategic advantage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adoption, unit economics, risk controls | Realised benefits, reuse, retention | Margin, growth, defensibility |

**Each milestone needs an owner, a date, a baseline, a metric and a gate.**

### Design the board pack for decisions

- **Lead with the answer**: recommendation and precise ask on page one.
- **Use assertion headlines**: "Release tranche 2 only after value clears the gate."
- **One message per slide**: put detailed calculations in the appendix.
- **Show uncertainty honestly**: label estimates, scenarios and assumptions.
- **Make numbers traceable**: units, period, baseline, owner and source.

### The three hardest board questions

| Question | Answer with |
| --- | --- |
| What is the worst case? | Quantify the downside; cap the loss |
| What kills it? | A metric, threshold, date and owner |
| What does the competitor do? | The cost of waiting and your advantage |

**Six tests the defence is scored on**: (1) decision and ask are explicit; (2) the portfolio follows strategic logic; (3) economics and assumptions are auditable; (4) downside and kill gates are credible; (5) change and governance have owners; (6) answers are concise and evidence-led.

### The board-ready checklist

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Decision | What exactly must be approved? |
| Strategy | Why this portfolio, now? |
| Capital | Where does the money go, and when does it return? |
| Risk | What is the worst case, and who owns it? |
| Execution | Who changes work, and what proves progress? |
| Discipline | What stops, pauses or scales the programme? |

Deliverable: the 2-page Comex memo (executive summary) plus a detailed plan for each topic after it.

## 9. Practice questions with answers

Q1 is the deck's case; its answer comes from the debrief slide (p172). The other answers are suggestions that apply the course frameworks.

1. **Diagnose the plant.** An industrial firm's AI scheduling tool has been live for 9 months. Factory-floor adoption is 12%. Plant supervisors route work around the tool. The Comex gets a 40-page steering deck every month; floor staff got one launch e-mail. Which failure modes are at work? Which ADKAR stage is stuck? Give three 90-day actions with owners and signals.
    - **Middle-management sabotage**: supervisors route around the tool because it takes over the scheduling decisions that give them control. Supervisors are stuck at **Desire**.
    - **Cadence set up backwards**: 40 pages a month to the Comex; the floor got one e-mail and never heard why, or how. Operators are stuck at **Awareness and Knowledge**.
    - 90-day response: (1) supervisors co-design the scheduling rules, with targets relaxed for one quarter (owner: plant manager); (2) weekly 15-minute floor huddles led by supervisors, two champions per shift (owner: operations excellence lead); (3) swap the 40-page deck for a one-page monthly dashboard (owner: Chief AI Officer); (4) kill criterion: below 40% floor adoption at month 12 → redesign or retire (owner: COO).
2. **A memo says: "We will train all 8,000 staff in Q1, then roll out; adoption will follow." Critique it.**
    - It treats adoption as a Knowledge problem. Without Awareness and Desire (the why, the WIIFM, a redeployment promise), training won't stick. It also has no tiers (literacy vs practitioner vs executive), no in-the-flow practice, no champions, and no measurement of usage → behaviour → value. Fix: CEO story, manager-led WIIFM, three tiers, a champions network, and adoption metrics with a kill criterion.
3. **Turn "We will monitor adoption closely" into a proper kill criterion.**
    - "If weekly active users are below 40% of target at month 12, tranche 2 is paused and the CFO, as portfolio owner, decides to redesign or retire the initiative." That has a metric, threshold, date, owner and action, agreed before launch.
4. **Write the first line of a Comex memo for MaisonRetail.**
    - "Approve a EUR 30M, 3-year AI envelope (tranche 1: EUR 11M today) for an H1-led 70-20-10 portfolio that clears our 12% hurdle on expected value (EV IRR 17.0%, ENPV +1.9M), with downside capped by stage gates at months 12 and 24; doing nothing costs an estimated EUR 8M in PV."
5. **A board member asks: "What if our two best AI engineers leave?" Answer.**
    - That is talent flight, owned by the CHRO + Chief AI Officer. The signal we watch is attrition and declined offers in AI roles. Mitigations: AI career paths and pay bands, retention terms, putting them on H2/H3 work rather than tickets, protected learning time, and documented, platform-based delivery (hub-and-spoke) so knowledge does not sit in two heads.
6. **Where do the works council and legal sit on the influence grid, and why does it matter?**
    - They are guardians: high power, low interest, so "keep satisfied" until something goes wrong. Move them to "manage closely" before the pilot. Consult them early and put consultation on the critical path; Nanterre showed a court can suspend the tools.
