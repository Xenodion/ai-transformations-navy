# Change management plan

**Supporting decision paper | Internal proposal to the CEO and Board | 11 November 2026**

Branches already own their AI projects, but each project is split across four reporting lines: the branch project lead, a branch data engineer who does not report to that lead, a Digital Factory (TDF) data scientist without a financial objective, and an end user without decision power. We form a dedicated trio on each project (TDF data scientist, branch data engineer and business expert) working together for a large share of their time, reporting day to day to the branch project lead and keeping line reporting to their own managers. Objectives and continuation gates are unchanged; work moves faster, decision-makers steer more projects in parallel and AI practice spreads into the branches. Training, protected time and backfill are funded within the project envelopes, or the case is repriced.

## Page 1 — Risks, early warning and ownership

**Specialist capacity and retention — CHRO and Digital Factory lead.** Monitor vacancies, unfilled secondments and single points of failure monthly. Record a named deputy and handover plan for each trio member before pilots begin; cross-train and reschedule deployments if capacity is unavailable. Line managers keep staffing authority and agree each secondment.

**Manager and frontline adoption — branch sponsors and site managers.** Track protected expert time, attendance at pilot reviews, active use and operator feedback. When a team cannot release its expert, the branch sponsor arranges backfill within the allowance or returns for a revised scope and budget. No deployment displaces safety procedures or human operational authority.

**Dual-reporting friction — project leads and line managers.** Agree each trio member's objectives jointly before launch: project objectives with the project lead, professional standards with the line manager. Conflicts on priorities escalate to the branch sponsor within a week.

**Governance and employee trust — General Counsel, DPO, HSE and employee-relations leads.** Before pilot release, assess data rights, security, privacy, operational safety and required employee-representative consultation. Missing clearance or an unsafe incident pauses the affected deployment immediately; the relevant control owner determines remediation and approves restart.

**Premature cancellation or escalation — CFO and portfolio sponsor.** Review the investment trough (expected −$10.2m at end Y1) alongside spend, adoption and leading operating indicators quarterly. Follow the agreed gates instead of cancelling solely because early cash flow is negative; stop or redesign when prospective economics, safety or contractual conditions fail.

## Page 2 — Stakeholders and communication

**Sponsors:** CEO, CFO and branch leaders set the mandate and resolve capacity or funding conflicts. The portfolio sponsor sends a monthly one-page dashboard showing spend, milestones, risk and next decision; material incidents escalate immediately.

**Translators:** branch project leads and managers translate each initiative into schedules, roles and performance measures. They review trio capacity and adoption every two weeks, resolve friction and protect expert time.

**Adopters:** site operators, maintenance specialists, drilling teams, retail staff and other pilot users shape the workflow through the business expert and test recommendations. The trio runs short weekly two-way sessions during a pilot, logs concerns and publishes decisions to the team. Users retain their operational decision rights and a manual fallback.

**Guardians:** HSE, cyber, Legal, DPO, procurement and employee representatives are engaged during scoping, and required consultation is complete before deployment. They receive the intended workflow, data, safeguards, effect on work and escalation route; the outcome and owner are recorded.

## Page 3 — Skills, measurement and stop rules

**Three training tiers.** (1) For affected staff: practical AI literacy, acceptable data use, uncertainty and escalation. (2) For trio members, practitioners and managers: workflow design, validation, human oversight and interpretation of performance. (3) For executives: scenario assumptions, portfolio risk, adoption and gate decisions. The CHRO and branch sponsors set audiences, completion measures and backfill before launch, within the project envelopes.

**Adoption staircase.** Months 0–6: usage (eligible, trained and active users). Months 6–12: behaviour (decisions recorded through the tool, cycle time, user feedback). From month 12: value (Finance-verified incremental cash against the baseline). Delivery-model measures run alongside: interval between progress reviews, lead time to first live use, expert time available and projects per lead. For projects without a user workflow, technical and commercial milestones replace user metrics.

**Release discipline.** User-facing pilots with fewer than **40% of eligible users active** in the month before the month-6 review pause expansion and agree remediation or stop within 30 days. At month 12, continuation requires at least **60% of eligible decisions recorded** through the tool, alongside the project gate. Safety, legal and contractual failures trigger an immediate pause. At months 12, 24 and 36 the steering group decides to scale, redesign or stop each initiative; no milestone guarantees further funding.
