# AI value programme: small, gated bets that turn AI in production into audited cash

**To:** Patrick Pouyanné, Chairman and CEO, and the Executive Committee · **From:** Team 4 · **Meeting:** 16 November 2026

**Decision requested:**
- authorise an AI value programme of up to **$19.8M over its launch year and three years** (CAPEX $7.3M, OPEX $12.5M);
- commit **wave 1**: four focus cases and the value register, **$10.2M**‡;
- release **tranche 1 ($6.7M**‡**)** today;
- decide everything else at evidence gates, starting at **month 12 (November 2027)**.

## 1. Thesis: we are past pilots, but no value is measured yet

Invest up to $19.8M in 14 initiatives led by four focus cases. In the base case this creates **+$3.7M of NPV** at project hurdles of 9–12%, after tax: +$3.3M probability-weighted. The base case breaks even in **year 4**. Exposure is limited by stop gates, and the first release decision is at month 12.

**Why now:**
- **AI already runs at scale.** The Digital Factory has about 500 experts, close to 100 solutions and more than 10,000 live AI models (URD 2025, p. 42).
- **The data foundations are funded.** The Cognite and AspenTech platforms land across upstream and industrial sites in 2027–2028, and Mistral is a programme of more than €100M.
- **No value is measured.** We publish no measured value for any solution. Our risk factors name the pace of AI adoption as a risk, and the Board plans to review AI's impact in 2026 (URD 2025, pp. 138, 225).
- **Peers measure what we don't.** Equinor reports $130M of AI value for 2025, partners included and method undisclosed. Shell reports a 70% fall in safety observations with its T-Pulse system. The gap is in measurement, not proven performance.
- **We are under pressure on two fronts.** We are delivering $12.5bn of savings over 2026–2030. AI literacy is a legal duty since February 2025, and high-risk AI obligations apply from 2 December 2027 (Reg. (EU) 2026/1744).

**Working diagnosis, tested at the first gates:** value is held back less by models or platforms than by **delivery into operations and measurement of the result**. Each case is therefore run by its business owner, data specialists and frontline users together, and Finance signs every benefit.

**The numbers are deliberately modest.** Each case is valued on a bounded pilot: two refining sites, two rigs or campaigns, 250 MW, 100 stations. Any scale-up is a later decision taken on measured results, and no value from it is counted here.

*We ask this meeting to approve the programme ceiling and tranche 1, name a Comex sponsor, and set the month-12 gate.*

## 5. Financial analysis: tested against our own cost of capital

- **Hurdles:** our impairment tests use a **7% post-tax WACC** (URD 2025, p. 472). We add 2–3 points by segment and up to 3 more points by project, giving **9–12%**. Cash flows are after tax (25% assumed), over 5 years, with no terminal value.
- **Base case:** NPV +$3.7M. IRR 20%‡ on the combined cash flows, against hurdles of 9–12%.
- **J-curve:** the trough is −$11.8M at the end of year 1; break-even comes in year 4.
- **Downside:** the IRR falls to 6%‡, below the WACC. That is why capital is released in steps.
- **Break-even benefit levels:** each focus case still breaks even at **51–58% of its assumed benefits**, except P11 at 91%. Gate thresholds will be set above these levels.
- **H3:** no payback is claimed.
- **Cost of inaction:** measured against continuing our current programmes, not against "zero". A one-year delay moves the whole J-curve a year later: [TBD: cost of waiting, $M PV]. We claim no competitor-driven erosion without evidence.

## 6. Risks and kill criteria: every release needs evidence first

| Gate | Evidence required | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| G0: eligibility (before launch) | Eligible sites, rigs or stations; existing solutions; data and contract rights; baseline; full pilot cost | Fund only the bounded pilot |
| G1: pilot (month [6–12]) | Measured outcome against the baseline; adoption; safety; costs incurred | Continue, extend or stop |
| G2: scaling (month 12 onwards) | Verified cash mechanism; attribution to TotalEnergies; full rollout and run costs | Release only the next increment |
| H3 learning | Novelty, customer take-up, technical and regulatory conditions | Continue the learning budget or stop |

- **Stop rule:** a stopped case keeps only its sunk and non-cancellable costs. Unrelated cases continue.
- **Owners:**
  - business owner and CFO: value;
  - Chief Digital Officer: delivery, data, cyber;
  - Legal and Risk: AI Act classification and human final decisions;
  - segment heads and CHRO: people and social dialogue.

## 7. Change management: value lands where work changes

- **Operating model:** we formalise the hub-and-spoke we already have.
  - **Hub:** the Digital Line and Digital Factory own platforms, standards, vendors and the value register.
  - **Spokes:** the segments own the use case and its benefit.
- **Roles per case:**
  - the branch sponsor owns the objective, funding and gate decision;
  - the operational lead owns safe use and changes to the workflow;
  - Finance sets the baseline and signs the realised benefits;
  - the data and IT lead owns data access, cyber and support.
- **People:** frontline users help design each case and are trained on it. Training and backfill time are costed, and employee representatives are consulted early.
- **Reporting:** a monthly one-page dashboard for the Comex, tracking usage, then behaviour, then cash, each reported separately.
- **Milestones:**
  - **M12:** baselines set and first measured results.
  - **M24:** proven cases extended.
  - **M36:** value audited in the register.

## 8. The ask

1. **Authorise** the programme ceiling of **$19.8M** (Y0–Y3) and **release tranche 1, $6.7M**‡, for wave 1: P01, P03, P11, P13 and the value register.
2. **Name a Comex sponsor** who owns the monthly dashboard and every release decision.
3. **Decide at month 12 (November 2027)** on the next increments, against the G1 and G2 evidence.
